Natural and Useful Proofs

Trustworthy and Useful

  • We talk a lot about proof verification and trust
  • Have we done enough to make proofs understandable (in context)?
  • If it is raining, I should take my umbrella. It is raining. Explain why I should take my umbrella.
    • LLMs answer naturally

      Whenever it is raining I should take my umbrella.
      It is raining, so the current situation falls under that rule.
      Therefore, I should take my umbrella.

    • ATPs answer unnaturally

      Assume that I do not take my umbrella.
      From that assumption we are led to the conclusion that it is not raining.
      But this contradicts the given fact that it is raining.
      Therefore the assumption that I do not take my umbrella must be false.
      So I should take my umbrella.

      WTF?!!?

A Call to Arms

  • A refutation is a proof that the conjecture is a theorem (not a proof of the theorem)
  • A closed tableau is a magical proof that conjecture is a theorem
  • A sequent calculus proof is an unnatural proof of the theorem
  • A natural deduction can be a natural proof of the theorem
  • An direct proof is a natural and useful proof ... all inferences are consequences
    • logical consequences
    • mathematical consequences
    • legal consequences
    • chemical consequences
    • physical consequences
    • political consequences
    • etc.

  • A "proof that the conjecture is a theorem" is useful for finding a "proof of the theorem".
